# SuperFinz Gig-Worker Spending Research — Canonical Source Report

**Audience:** SuperFinz hackathon team

**Research date:** 3 September 2026

**Geography:** India first; global comparators where useful
**Decision:** Which gig-worker spending/resilience problem SuperFinz should solve, and how it should differentiate

## Scope and assumptions

This research covers platform workers and adjacent informal/independent workers with irregular income. It focuses on spending control, cash-flow timing, savings flexibility, work-related costs, shocks, and responsible access to credit. It does not attempt a legal opinion, full market-sizing exercise, or exhaustive global product census.

## Direct answer

The best wedge is not another expense tracker, AI coach, fixed monthly budget, savings app, or instant-credit product. SuperFinz should become an **adaptive income firewall**: aggregate settled income across sources, subtract the costs required to keep earning, protect commitments through the next uncertain payout, and expose one explainable amount that is safe to use. At each actual inflow, a user-controlled priority waterfall should adapt allocations to current obligations instead of applying a rigid percentage. When a shortfall is forecast, the product should orchestrate non-credit actions before displaying any regulated-partner credit.

The combined public capabilities reviewed do not establish that this concept is globally unique. Generic safe-to-spend products already exist. The defensible difference is the India-specific combination of cross-platform income, cash/manual entries, true net earnings, payout ranges, adaptive commitment protection, workday scenarios, protected-days resilience, and a non-credit-first recovery path.

## Evidence synthesis

- India had an estimated 7.7 million gig workers in 2020–21 and NITI Aayog projected 23.5 million by 2029–30, while explicitly warning that the estimate is indicative because official data are limited.
- Dvara Research’s Bengaluru diaries study recorded 57% variation around average daily earnings and 79% around average daily work expenses. Its small sample averaged ₹35,075 gross platform income, ₹18,470 work expenses, and ₹16,604 net platform income.
- In Dvara’s 14-day diaries, 18 of 22 participants borrowed at least once; 26 of 44 new loans were for consumption smoothing, averaging ₹1,763. Formal fixed-contribution savings products did not fit well; participants valued flexible liquidity and mentally earmarked money for recurrent obligations.
- CGAP pilots found automated saving tied to actual digital earnings more usable than a manual extra step, but also found that in-person support materially improved adoption. This supports inflow-triggered, opt-in protection plus assisted onboarding.
- CFPB analysis of Qapital data found guaranteed saving rules associated with larger accumulation than spending-contingent rules, but this is US observational product data and not gig-worker-specific. It supports reliable inflow-based rules over round-up gimmicks, not a universal causal claim.
- Existing offerings solve fragments: platform payout visibility; EWA/credit; fixed-rule savings; financial-product distribution; tax/bookkeeping; generic safe-to-spend; or social-protection access.
- RBI’s Account Aggregator framework requires explicit consent, while its 2025 Digital Lending Directions require regulated-entity accountability, comparison disclosures, APR/KFS information, cooling-off, grievance handling, and need-based consented data collection. SuperFinz must partner rather than pretend these regulated functions are native.

## Material limitations

- The most detailed India diaries evidence is a 22-person, 14-day Bengaluru sample and is largely male; it is rich qualitative evidence, not a nationally representative estimate.
- The NITI workforce projection was published in 2022 and is expressly indicative.
- Product comparisons reflect public pages available on the research date, not hands-on audits, contract terms, or a complete market census.
- Current hackathon competitors are unknown. Statements about likely duplicate ideas are inference from the problem statement and product landscape.
- The proposed scoring and forecasting logic requires co-design and validation with workers before real financial decisions or underwriting use.

## Search record and stop rationale

Searches covered Indian gig workforce data, Indian financial diaries, global income-volatility evidence, automatic savings experiments/pilots, responsible digital-lending rules, Account Aggregator consent rules, e-Shram, Indian gig-fintech offerings, general Indian savings apps, global contractor banking, and generic safe-to-spend products. Research stopped after the core problem, behavioral mechanism, competitor categories, regulatory feasibility, and differentiating product wedge each had primary or credible first-party support; further broad searches were returning duplicate product categories rather than changing the recommendation.
